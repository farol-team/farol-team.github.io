The bottleneck

AI transformation stalls before it starts.

Most teams still discover automation opportunities the slow way: interviews, workshops, screenshots, sticky notes, and reconstructed workflows. That makes the first phase expensive, political, and easy to distort.

Interview bias

People describe what they think they do.

Not what they actually repeat fifty times per day across tabs, inboxes, documents, and side chats.

Process blind spots

System logs miss the manual last mile.

Copy-paste, comparison, reconciliation, handoffs, and ad hoc decision loops rarely show up in process mining.

Consulting drag

Senior time gets burned on raw discovery.

Your experts spend high-value hours collecting facts instead of judging, designing, and selling the transformation.

How Gilbreth Works

A capture layer for AI-native redesign.

Gilbreth starts narrow enough to demo, but points at a larger destination: the company brain. The visible output is candidate automation. The accumulating asset is context.

What we capture

The starting wedge is full-context observation of knowledge work: screen activity plus connected work streams such as mail, chat, calls, docs, and tasks. The goal is a faithful picture of how the work actually moves.

screen slack email calls docs tasks

What we output

Observed traces become artifacts of repeated work. Artifacts become candidate skills. Skills become inputs for the consultant's automation roadmap and for the longer-term company brain.

FAQ

The hard questions, early.

Trust, positioning, and change management are not side notes here. They are part of the product boundary.

Is this process mining?

No. Traditional process mining relies on system logs. Gilbreth is aimed at the messy, manual, cross-tool work that never cleanly enters those logs.

Does Gilbreth replace the consultant?

No. It removes raw discovery drag and produces candidate skills. Judgment, approval, narrative, and change management remain with the transformation lead.

Why emphasize trust so much?

Because any product that captures work can be mistaken for surveillance. We are deliberately building around consent, explicit scope, and a context-for-automation story rather than a productivity-policing story.
